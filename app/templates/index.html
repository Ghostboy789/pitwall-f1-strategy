The finding

What one place on track costs, in seconds

A car half a second a lap quicker than the one ahead does not simply drive past. It waits. How long it waits is a property of the circuit — and that wait, priced in seconds, is what track position is worth. Where the bar is long, an undercut that gains a place is worth keeping; where it is short, the place will come back anyway.

The hard part

Tyre degradation is measured from data that systematically hides it

Teams pit when the tyre is finished, so the worst of every degradation curve is never observed — and it is missing non-randomly, because a tyre going off early gets changed early. Worse, teams choose the harder compound for exactly the stints where degradation will be worst, so the compounds are never seen under equal conditions. Uncorrected, the data says harder tyres wear faster than softer ones, which is backwards.

Naive Two-way fixed effects + censoring correction

Median degradation across circuits, by the compound's rank within its own event — the only comparison the relative Soft/Medium/Hard labels support. The corrected estimator recovers an ordering it was never told about: softer tyres degrade faster.

Validation

Does the model deserve to be believed?

The overtaking model outputs probabilities, so being right on average is not enough — it has to be right at every confidence level. And an optimiser that claims to beat professional strategists by a wide margin is broken, not brilliant.
